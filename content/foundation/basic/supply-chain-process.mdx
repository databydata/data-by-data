# A Simple Supply Chain Scenario: From Order to Delivery

In the world of logistics, managing inbound and outbound operations is a complex dance of coordination. As a **Store Clerk**, you are often at the center of this process, ensuring goods move efficiently from the warehouse to the customer's door.

Let’s walk through a typical supply chain scenario to understand the lifecycle of an order.

## Phase 1: Order Processing & Planning

The journey begins with the customer.

1.  **Order Placement**: A customer places a request via your **CRM portal**.
2.  **Sales Order Creation**: As soon as you receive the **Purchase Order (PO)** from the customer, you create a corresponding **Sales Order** in your system.
3.  **Inventory Check**: Before proceeding, the system checks if the required stock is available in the warehouse.
4.  **Scheduling**: Once stock is confirmed, a **Delivery Date** is finalized.

## Phase 2: Transportation Planning

With the dates set, the focus shifts to how the goods will move.

* **Shipment Planning**: You plan the shipment based on the finalized delivery date.
* **Carrier Selection**: A carrier is selected to transport the goods. This selection can happen via:
    * Subcontracting
    * Tendering
    * Manual Selection
* **Cost Finalization**: The freight cost is agreed upon and finalized with the carrier.

## Phase 3: Warehouse Execution

Now, the physical work begins inside the facility.

1.  **Picking**: The items are picked from the warehouse shelves.
2.  **Packing**: The goods are brought to the packing area, where they are packed, and a final **Handling Unit (HU)** is created.
3.  **Staging**: The packed material is moved to the **Shipping Point**, ready for pickup.

## Phase 4: Yard & Dock Management

Coordination extends outside the warehouse walls to the yard.

* **Truck Arrival**: The carrier is instructed to bring the truck to a dedicated space in the yard.
* **Loading**: The driver brings the truck to the assigned **Door/Shipping Point**.
* **Departure**: Loading operations are carried out, documents are handed over, and the truck leaves the premise.

## Phase 5: Transit & Delivery

Once the truck is on the road, communication is key.

* **Exception Handling**: If the truck faces delays due to unforeseen reasons (e.g., natural calamity or traffic), the driver communicates this delay to all relevant parties instantly.
* **Proof of Delivery (POD)**: The driver arrives at the customer’s location, delivers the package, and generates a Proof of Delivery.
* **Settlement**: Finally, the payment is made to the carrier for their services.



## The Technology Stack: SAP Solutions

To manage this end-to-end flow efficiently, SAP provides specialized solutions for each distinct area of the supply chain:

**Warehouse** **SAP EWM** (Extended Warehouse Management) Manages picking, packing, and internal stock movements.  
**Transportation** **SAP TM** (Transportation Management) Handles carrier selection, freight planning, and cost calculation.  
**Yard** **SAP YL** (Yard Logistics) Manages truck check-ins, dock assignments, and yard movements.  
**Collaboration** **SAP BN4L** (Business Network for Logistics) Facilitates carrier subcontracting, tendering, and real-time driver communication.
